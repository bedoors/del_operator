How ▷ turned into ∇

Drag through the years. Hamilton’s sideways triangle swings upright in Tait’s hands, then picks up two names along the way.

  1. Hamilton’s operator

    William Rowan Hamilton writes a vector differential operator into his work on quaternions, drawn as a sideways triangle. Quaternions turned out to be a natural language for rotation.

    ▷

  2. Quaternions in print

    Hamilton’s Lectures on Quaternions works out what the operator does to scalar and to vector quantities.

    ▷f(q)

  3. The familiar ∇

    Peter Guthrie Tait’s quaternion treatise helps establish the inverted triangle we still write today.

    ∇

  4. A language for fields

    Maxwell, Gibbs and Heaviside carry these operators into physics and modern vector analysis. Maxwell proposes the name “curl” in 1871.

    ∇·  ∇×

  5. “Del” gets a textbook

    Edwin B. Wilson’s Vector Analysis, built from Gibbs’s lectures, promotes the name “del”.
